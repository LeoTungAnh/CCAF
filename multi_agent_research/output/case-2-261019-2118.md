All I have from it is its scope, a meta-analysis of 47 studies on orthopedic, cardiac and neurological surgery. The report says this and doesn't guess at its results.

**Findings, by area:**
- **Diagnosis (radiology):** In a randomized trial at 15 hospitals with 12,847 patients, radiologists reading chest X-rays with AI help were 94.2% accurate, against 87.5% without it. The AI alone scored 91.8%. Time per case halved, from 4.2 to 2.1 minutes. Only one commercial system was tested, and accuracy wasn't broken down by patient group.
- **Bias:** A JAMA review of 15 commercial clinical decision support tools used in US hospitals found that 9 performed differently across racial groups. A dermatology tool was 31% less accurate on darker skin. Only 3 of the 15 vendors publish results by patient group. Given this, the radiology accuracy figures may not hold equally for all patients.
- **Drug discovery:** Nature Biotechnology reports that machine-learning screening cut the time to find a drug candidate from 4.5 to 2.7 years, about 40% faster. I only have the search excerpt for this one.
- **Costs and operations:** AI-based remote monitoring cut 30-day hospital readmissions by 23%, saving an estimated $4.2M per hospital a year. This comes from a trade publication, not a peer-reviewed journal, and I only have the excerpt.
